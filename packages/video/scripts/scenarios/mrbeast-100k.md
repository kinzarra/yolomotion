# MrBeast Counted To 100,000

- **id**: mrbeast-100k
- **format**: reel (1080×1920), 50.5s
- **style**: fast-paced mini-documentary + data visualisation. Black frame,
  huge kinetic type, tabular-mono counters, a mock YouTube card, an assembling
  formula, a rising graph.
- **hero color**: YouTube red `#FF1E28` — one red element per frame, everything
  else white / grey on near-black. No second signal color.
- **hook (first 2s)**: black, **40 HOURS** slams in, hard cut to **COUNTING.**
  while a counter spins through 98,4xx behind it.
- **language**: English

## Beats

1. **hook** (3.5s) — 40 HOURS → hard cut → COUNTING. + spinning counter,
   archival player card with the source label.
2. **count** (5s) — counter accelerates 1 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000 → 100,000,
   camera pushes in, impact + screen shake on 100,000.
3. **stamp** (5.5s) — freeze-frame, red stamp **STUPID IDEA?** → beat →
   stamp transforms into **GENIUS FORMAT.**
4. **thumbnail** (6s) — mock YouTube card with a 100,000 thumbnail, cursor
   hovers, three questions stack up.
5. **formula** (8.5s) — SIMPLE IDEA + EXTREME EXECUTION = IRRESISTIBLE STORY;
   on EXTREME the type floods the frame.
6. **timeline** (7s) — ladder: 2017 · COUNT TO 100,000 ↓ CHALLENGES ↓
   MILLIONS OF VIEWS ↓ MRBEAST, each rung a stylised era card.
7. **lesson** (7.5s) — everything clears to black; MAKE / THE IDEA /
   IMPOSSIBLE / TO IGNORE land one word at a time.
8. **yoloco** (7.5s) — cursor → thumbnail → CLICK, the graph shoots up,
   resolves into Yoloco branding.

Pacing: cuts 1–4 are fast and loud, 5–6 are the "documentary" middle, 7 is the
deliberate silence-and-black contrast the piece is built around, 8 is the turn.

## Voiceover

- `01-hook`: MrBeast once spent forty hours... just counting.
- `02-count`: He sat in front of a camera and counted all the way to one
  hundred thousand.
- `03-stamp`: It sounds like the dumbest YouTube idea imaginable. But there was
  something genius about it.
- `04-thumbnail`: Because you understand the entire video — before you even
  click it.
- `05-formula`: And that's the trick MrBeast would eventually master. Take
  something ridiculously simple, and push it to an extreme.
- `06-timeline`: Counting became bigger challenges. Bigger challenges became
  bigger videos. And bigger videos built MrBeast.
- `07-lesson`: The lesson isn't "do something stupid". It's this. Make the idea
  impossible to ignore.
- `08-yoloco`: Because before the algorithm can make you viral... a human still
  has to click.

The pages for "40 HOURS / COUNTING", "ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND" and the whole
lesson beat are hidden (`~`) — the scene headlines carry those words.

## Footage

The scenario asks for archival MrBeast shots. Rather than borrow unlicensed
footage or synthesise a likeness, the reel uses two photographs that are
genuinely free to use, both from Wikimedia Commons (see
`public/images/CREDITS.md`):

- **`mrbeast-tape.jpg`** — a frame from a Fidias video, CC BY 3.0. Rebuilt as a
  16:9 plate (subject bottom-centre over a blurred, darkened copy of itself)
  because the 3:4 source has no head-and-shoulders 16:9 crop. It carries the
  archival beats 01, 02, 03 and the 2017 rung of 06.
- **`mrbeast-cutout.png`** — a studio portrait by Steven Khan, CC BY 4.0, cut
  off its sweep by `scripts/cutout.mjs` and given the white die-cut border a
  paper clipping has. It is the collage element: the subject of the mock
  thumbnail in beats 04 and 08 (opposite the number, which is how a real
  MrBeast thumbnail is composed), and the full-height figure the MRBEAST
  wordmark lands on in beat 06.

Both are graded to greyscale at render time so a photograph never spends the
frame's single hero colour, and the cut-out carries a hard, unblurred offset
shadow — a clipping sits on the page rather than glowing above it. CC BY requires credit, so the closing beat carries
an on-screen line naming both photographers, both licences and the source —
keep it in any re-cut.
